title: "Redemptions"
description: "How a protocol redemption against the shared trove is split, value-neutrally, and the H-2 boundary."
---

MUSD lets anyone redeem MUSD for BTC against the lowest-collateralised troves, at par. When that
happens to the pooled trove, the trove loses collateral and sheds debt in one event, and the
vault has to divide both sides across its users fairly. That is the job of the **redemption
splitter**.

## What a redemption does to the pool

A redemption removes collateral from the shared trove and cancels an equal value of its debt.
Neither side belongs to any single user, so the splitter distributes both across debt shares: the
collateral removed is charged pro rata, and the debt relief is credited pro rata.

<Info>
  The splitter is **value-neutral** by design. A user charged some BTC of collateral should
  receive an equal value of debt relief, so a redemption changes what a user holds but not what it
  is worth. The audit's regression tests assert this neutrality holds for a solvent debtor, both
  on a plain redemption and on one arriving after a recognised shortfall.
</Info>

The relevant functions are `reconcileRedemption`, `_syncState`, `redemptionCollateralPerShare`,
`settleRedemption`, and `collateralOf`. They were the primary target of the
[contracts audit](/security/audits).

## The bug that was found and fixed: H-1

The first version of the splitter had a High-severity flaw. When a redemption produced a
**shortfall** (an uncollectable debit from a position below 100% collateralisation), the contract
correctly recorded the shortfall rather than papering over it, but then re-read that same
recorded shortfall on the next sync as if it were a brand new redemption.

<Warning>
  The effect was a phantom redemption charged pro rata to every solvent debtor, repeatedly, with
  no debt relief attached, until the shortfall was fully socialised onto the solvent users. Both
  entrypoints that drive it are permissionless and need no price, so any passer-by could grind the
  loop. In the measured reproduction, about 0.0336 BTC (roughly $2,122) was drained from a solvent
  debtor with nothing given in return.
</Warning>

The fix routes both the detector and the preview through one function that subtracts the recorded
shortfall before comparing the books against the trove, saturating so it cannot underflow. Two
regression tests, both proven to fail against the pre-fix contract, pin it: one grinds both
permissionless entrypoints ten times and asserts no phantom is booked, the other asserts a genuine
redemption arriving after a shortfall is still measured at full size.

## The boundary that is documented, not fixed: H-2

One High-severity edge is **documented and left for the paid audit**, deliberately, with a test
pinning it.

The mechanism: debt relief is capped by the vault's MUSD reserve (`outstandingDebt = troveNetDebt
- reserveMUSD`), but the collateral debit is not. When a redemption cancels more trove debt than
the pool's users actually owe, which needs a non-zero reserve and a large redemption, users are
charged collateral for relief that lands in the reserve, where no user has a direct claim on it.

<Note>
  **The loss is bounded**: at most `reserveMUSD` at the instant the redemption lands, because the
  excess relief is exactly the part the reserve was already covering. In the measured case, a
  25,000 MUSD reserve against a trove owing 15,040 MUSD produced about 9,959 MUSD of value taken
  with nothing given for it, and the surplus sat in the reserve credited to nobody.
</Note>

## Why H-2 is left for the auditor

Every available fix decides **who** absorbs the excess, which is a governance question rather than
a rounding one:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Charge the excess to the protocol's own position" icon="building-columns">
    Coherent, since the excess relief accrued to the protocol-controlled reserve. This is the
    change that makes the value-neutrality claim strictly true. The tradeoff is that it can
    exhaust the protocol's own position and fall into the shortfall path.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Redistribute the surplus reserve to the debtors who paid for it" icon="arrows-split-up-and-left">
    Also coherent, but it needs a second per-share accumulator and more moving parts, which is
    itself new surface for the paid audit to review.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Warning>
  Because the choice is a governance decision with real tradeoffs, it is left for the paid audit
  rather than resolved unilaterally, and a test pins the current behaviour so it cannot drift
  unnoticed. This is the honest state: a bounded, documented High, not a silent one.
</Warning>
