Maren is a money app for Bitcoin, built on [Mezo](https://mezo.org). Deposit Bitcoin, borrow
MUSD against it from as little as 0.001 BTC, and spend it: pay a `@name`, send a money link,
request money, and put idle MUSD to work. Your collateral backs the loan and can be liquidated
if its value falls too far. Repay the debt to withdraw the remaining collateral.

Use the [web app](https://www.marenfinance.xyz/app) with a browser wallet on Mezo
testnet, or join the iOS TestFlight beta. Payment-link claims also work in the browser.
Borrowing depends on both your collateral and available capacity in the shared pool;
a healthy personal position does not guarantee that the pool can extend another loan.

<Warning>
  **Maren is pre-audit and lives on Mezo testnet only.** Every contract runs on Mezo matsnet (chain
  `31611`), and its mainnet addresses are all zeroes because Maren is not on mainnet: a third-party
  audit is the deliberate gate. Use testnet funds only. The gasless relayer is hosted and sponsoring
  transactions on matsnet, but a BTC collateral deposit carries native value and cannot be relayed.
  BTC deposits, link creation and approvals can require your own BTC gas.
</Warning>

## Why Maren

Mezo already runs the hard part: a live, audited engine where BTC mints a dollar. What it does
not have is a consumer door. The protocol requires a 2,000 MUSD minimum loan (about $3,000 of BTC
to open responsibly), every action costs BTC gas, and once you hold MUSD there is almost nowhere
to spend it. On-chain, MUSD is a DeFi-internal asset. Read on Mezo mainnet on 2026-09-07,
`getEntireSystemDebt()` was 1.07M against a 30.55M supply — about 3.5% of the token was created
by user borrowing, across fewer than thirty troves.

Maren's edge is not cheaper collateral. It is **no minimum loan size at all**. You can borrow $25
against $77 of Bitcoin, which the protocol itself cannot do. Whether that demand exists is
unproven, and we treat it as a bet, not a certainty.

## Where we're building toward

Mobile-first Bitcoin spending, shipped in the order that keeps user funds safe:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Now: live on testnet">
    Five source-verified contracts on matsnet, and the iOS app in open TestFlight beta reading and
    writing live against them. The gasless relayer is hosted. The web client is built and runs
    locally but is switched off in production during the beta. A real user has borrowed through the
    app.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Next: verified testnet pilot">
    Verify installation and restoration on the distributed iOS build, complete the transaction loop,
    and gather independent tester feedback. Exercise relayer recovery across restarts and confirm
    the deployed contracts match the reviewed release.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Then: audit">
    An independent audit including MarenVault, MarenEarn and MarenRelayHub, with findings and
    remediations published. This is the gate to mainnet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Then: capped mainnet">
    Mainnet launch with a hard-capped pooled vault. The cap rises only as the position proves out.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The full trajectory, with the honest state of each phase, is on the [Roadmap](/get-started/roadmap).

## What Maren is not

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Not a claim of pent-up demand" icon="chart-line">
    We do not argue that thousands of idle MUSD holders are waiting to borrow. Borrowing on Mezo is
    barely used and whale-dominated. Maren is a demand-creation bet on a small, early chain, and the
    honest evidence for it is Mezo's off-chain community, not any on-chain count.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Sponsorship depends on the action" icon="gas-pump">
    The ERC-2771 relayer is hosted on testnet and sponsors eligible calls while available. Deposits,
    approvals when needed, and link creation still require BTC gas. See
    [Gasless](/how-it-works/gasless).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Not a savings account" icon="piggy-bank">
    Earn is an ERC-4626 wrapper over the MUSD Stability Pool. Its returns come from real liquidation
    gains, which are lumpy. Maren shows trailing realised yield and never advertises a forward APY,
    because there is no rate to guarantee.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
