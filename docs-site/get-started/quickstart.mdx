title: "Quickstart"
description: "Connect on matsnet, deposit BTC, borrow MUSD, and spend it."
---

The whole product loop on Mezo testnet: fund an account, deposit Bitcoin, borrow MUSD against it,
and pay someone. Every step runs against the five contracts live on matsnet and the real MUSD
protocol underneath.

<Info>
  Everything here happens on **Mezo matsnet, chain `31611`**. There is no real money at risk,
  and there is no Maren mainnet deployment to point at. Testnet BTC is the gas token, same as
  mainnet.
</Info>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connect a wallet on matsnet">
    Add the Mezo matsnet network to your wallet and switch to it. The primary RPC is
    `https://rpc.test.mezo.org`; a second endpoint is listed on [Networks](/reference/networks)
    for failover. The native gas token is **BTC with 18 decimals**, not 8, because Mezo is an
    EVM chain and BTC is its gas token.

    <Warning>
      Several official Mezo doc pages list RPC hosts that do not resolve (`rpc.mezo.org`,
      `testnet-rpc.mezo.org`). Use the endpoints on the [Networks](/reference/networks) page,
      each verified with a live `cast chain-id` rather than copied from a doc.
    </Warning>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Fund the account with a little testnet BTC">
    You need a small amount of matsnet BTC for gas. Your first deposit is a transaction you submit
    and pay for yourself, always: see the note below.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Deposit Bitcoin into the Social Vault">
    Deposit from as little as **0.001 BTC**. The vault holds one shared MUSD trove and gives
    you a proportional claim inside it, which is what lets you borrow below the protocol's
    minimum loan size. Collateral is native BTC passed as `msg.value`, so there is no ERC-20
    approval step.

    <Note>
      **Your first deposit costs BTC gas, always.** The relayer deliberately excludes
      `MarenVault.deposit()` from its sponsored allowlist, because a gasless relay cannot carry
      value. Everything you do after the deposit can be sponsored once the relayer is hosted.
      See [Gasless](/how-it-works/gasless).
    </Note>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Borrow MUSD, inside the range the contract will accept">
    Drag the borrow slider. It is bounded by the same computation the deployed contract
    enforces, in the contract's own order, so an amount you can select is an amount that
    clears. Maren refuses new borrowing below a **180% collateral ratio**, far above the
    protocol's 110% liquidation threshold, and marks a recommended safe level at **250%**.
    Nobody pays gas to discover a "no". See [Preflight](/how-it-works/preflight).

    A real user did exactly this on 17 August 2026: they deposited BTC and drew **25.7 MUSD**
    in a single transaction. At the same position a 40 MUSD borrow reverts with
    `InsufficientCollateralRatio` at the account's own 180% floor.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Spend the MUSD">
    Now the MUSD is yours to move.

    <Tabs>
      <Tab title="Pay a name">
        Register a handle through commit-reveal, then pay `@amaka` with an optional on-chain
        memo. Raw addresses work too. See [Pay](/product/pay).
      </Tab>
      <Tab title="Send a money link">
        Put MUSD into a claimable link and send it anywhere text goes. The recipient claims it
        with or without a Maren account and needs no BTC. Unclaimed links can be cancelled and
        refunded.
      </Tab>
      <Tab title="Request money">
        Ask for an amount with a note, share it as a link, and the payer settles in two taps.
        Both parties see the settled request in Activity with the transaction hash attached.
      </Tab>
    </Tabs>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Check it in Activity">
    Every action you took is a row in [Activity](/product/activity), read from contract logs,
    each with a real transaction hash linked to `explorer.test.mezo.org`. There is no sample
    data anywhere in the product.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Repay and walk away

You can always exit: repay your share of the pooled debt and withdraw your Bitcoin. If the pooled
trove is ever under stress, borrowing halts before withdrawals do, so exits stay open when
entries close. You are never trapped in a position you want out of.
